# 🏛️ Heist Architect V2: Deep Dive into Adversarial Reinforcement Learning

## 🎯 Core Philosophy: The Asymmetric Zero-Sum Game
At the heart of the **Heist Architect** is a concept known as asymmetric co-evolution. Typical Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents are trained against a static, pre-programmed environment. In our framework, the environment itself is dynamically generated by a competing Neural Network. 

This creates a pure **zero-sum game**. The two agents—the Architect and the Robber—are locked in an infinite arms race. The only way the Architect can increase its reward is by forcing the Robber to fail. The only way the Robber can succeed is by discovering blind spots in the Architect's defensive layouts. As one network patches a vulnerability, it forces the other network to discover a new, more advanced strategy, driving intelligence upwards without human intervention.

---

## 🧠 The Architect: Designing the Impenetrable Grid
The Architect is responsible for the defense. It does not control an avatar; rather, it controls the physical dimensions and layouts of the board before the game even begins.

*   **Action Space & Capabilities:** The Architect acts at the start of a rollout. It receives a randomly seeded maze of walls and empty space. It must then output probability distributions to decide exactly where to place interactive entities:
    *   **Cameras:** Placed on grid coordinates (flattened $20x20$ plane).
    *   **Guards:** Not just determining their starting points, but predicting sequence directions to establish looping patrol routes.
*   **The Objective Mechanics:** The Architect's reward function is the direct inverse of the Robber's. If a Robber enters a camera's Field of View, the Architect receives a $+1.0$ surveillance reward. If a guard catches a Robber, the Architect receives a massive $+10.0$ apprehension reward.
*   **Architecture Details:** The Architect Neural Network is built upon a Deep CNN structure packed with Residual Blocks (`ResBlock`) using Group Normalization. Because its output action space is enormous (predicting coordinates over a 400-cell grid for multiple cameras simultaneously), it requires dual output heads mapping from a massive 512-dimensional LSTM hidden state.

---

## 🥷 The Robber: Navigating the Gauntlet
The Robber is the active "Solver." It physically drops into the Architect's maze and must navigate in real-time.

*   **Action Space & State Perception:** The Robber moves across a discrete temporal axis (Up, Down, Left, Right, or Wait). To do this, it processes a dense 12-channel spatial tensor. It "sees" walls, vaults, the position of guards, and artificially generated heuristic channels like normalized geodesic distances and danger zones.
*   **Survival Mechanics:** The Robber fights against time and security.
    *   *Time Penalties:* A $-0.01$ constant decay forces efficiency and prevents the agent from hiding infinitely.
    *   *Alarm Penalties:* $-1.0$ for tripping a camera's static Field of View (FOV).
    *   *Apprehension:* $-10.0$ for stepping into a cell occupied by a guard, resulting in immediate episode termination.
    *   *Sub-goals:* Stealing a single vault rewards $+10.0$. Stealing the final vault and clearing the level rewards $+20.0$.
*   **Temporal Memory:** Because guards move dynamically, a static CNN cannot solve the environment; it cannot tell which direction a guard is walking from a single frame. The Robber employs a massive **512-parameter LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) cell**, constantly detaching and passing its hidden states (`hx, cx`) across timesteps to memorize guard patrol patterns.

## ⚙️ The Engine: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) 
Training two highly complex agents against each other using raw Policy Gradients would result in catastrophic vanishing gradients. Thus, this framework uses **PPO**, the industry-standard algorithm used in models like ChatGPT and OpenAI Five.

1. **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE):** Rather than just looking at the final reward, the Actor-Critic networks use an exponentially weighted look-ahead (controlled by $\lambda = 0.95$ and $\gamma = 0.99$) to calculate the *advantage* of a move compared to what the network *thought* the outcome would be.
2. **The Surrogate Clipped Objective:** In adverserial environments, if the Architect discovers an amazing layout, its gradients might update its weights so aggressively that it destroys the rest of its layout knowledge. PPO's core innovation—the ratio clipping defined by `clip_eps: 0.2`—mathematically prevents the neural network from altering its intelligence by more than 20% in a single update step. This ensures monotonic, stable learning.
3. **Entropy Regularization:** To prevent both models from prematurely finding a boring Nash Equilibrium (e.g., the Robber just standing still to avoid guards), we calculate the entropy of the network's predictive distributions and add it as a bonus back to the loss function (`entropy_coef: 0.02`). This statistically forces the models to continuously explore chaotic and strange new tactics.

## ⚔️ Preventing Strategy Collapse: The Self-Play Pool
A notorious problem in multi-agent RL systems is **Strategy Collapse**. 
*   *The Problem:* The Architect might learn a dominant strategy (A), causing the Robber to learn a counter-strategy (B). The Architect then forgets (A) to learn a counter-counter-strategy (C). Over 10,000 episodes, the Architect might eventually cycle back and have entirely forgotten how to use strategy (A).
*   *The Solution:* We implemented a `SelfPlayPool`. Every 200 episodes, we freeze a carbon copy of the Architect and Robber weights and store them in memory.
*   When executing an episode, the framework rolls a die (`selfplay_use_latest = 0.7`). 70% of the time, the Robber fights the bleeding-edge Architect. But 30% of the time, it is randomly dropped into an arena against a historical snapshot of the Architect from thousands of episodes ago. This ensures the agents never "forget" how to defeat older strategies.

## 🎓 Continuous Scaling: The Curriculum Manager
If we started training with 8 cameras and 4 guards immediately, the untrained Robber would be caught within 5 steps every time. Because it never sees a reward for grabbing a vault, its neural gradients would die out, and learning would plateau at zero.

To solve the sparse reward problem, we use **Curriculum Learning**:
1. **Rookie Stage:** The setup begins trivially easy (2 cameras, 1 guard). The Robber quickly learns rudimentary navigation and the mapping of vaults.
2. **Dynamic Advancement:** The system tracks the Robber's win-rate over a rolling 100-episode window. The threshold is purely mathematical (`win_thresh: 0.55`).
3. **Ascension:** Only when the Robber demonstrates statistical dominance over the Architect (winning >55% of the time) does the framework physically restructure the environment, moving the agents into "Intermediate", "Expert", and "Master" configurations.

## ⚡ Infrastructure: True Dual-GPU Asynchrony
Modern RL frameworks typically utilize `nn.DataParallel` to split the computation of identical models across GPUs. However, the Architect and Robber are fundamentally different architectures with varying parameter sizes (8.5M vs. 7M). 

We employ **True Split Asynchronous Deployment**:
*   The entire Architect PPO Agent, buffer, and ResNet block physicalize on `cuda:0`.
*   The entire Robber PPO Agent, CNN, and LSTM block physicalize on `cuda:1`.
By severing the GPU workloads entirely, each agent retains access to their own dedicated ~15GB VRAM pool, preventing Out-Of-Memory interrupts and allowing the processing of millions of sequential tensor steps simultaneously.
